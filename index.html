01

The Setup — Vessel, Pour & First Observations

Learning Objectives
  • Choose the right vessel for tasting mezcal
  • Observe perlas (bubbles) to gauge ABV
  • Understand why resting the pour matters

Choosing Your Vessel

Pour about 1 oz of mezcal neat into a traditional veladora — the small, wide-mouthed glass most commonly used across Mexico. Alternatively, a Glencairn whiskey glass works beautifully: its tulip shape concentrates aromatics while letting you swirl without spilling.

Reading the Perlas

As you pour (or shake the sealed bottle), watch for perlas — tiny bubbles that form on the surface. This is how mezcaleros have traditionally gauged the alcohol content. Longer-lasting bubbles generally indicate higher ABV.

Let It Breathe

Just like wine, oxygen opens up the flavors and aromatics of mezcal, letting the taste stabilize and soften. Give it a few minutes before your first sip. But be careful — too much air will cause mezcal to go flat and watery over time.

Pro Tip

When a bottle is less than half full, use a wine preserver spray (nitrogen-based) to displace oxygen and keep your mezcal fresh indefinitely.

02

The Senses — Smell, Color & Touch

Learning Objectives
  • Master the swirl-and-smell technique
  • Read color and viscosity for quality cues
  • Learn the traditional "touch" ritual

Swirl & Smell

Gently swirl the mezcal and observe the legs (streams that run down the glass). Hold the vessel level with your nose just above the rim — not plunged inside. Let the aromatics come to you. You'll pick up notes you'd miss by diving in too aggressively.

Color & Viscosity

Unaged mezcal (joven) should be crystal clear. Any color in an unaged expression is a red flag. The legs you observed while swirling often hint at the mouthfeel — thicker legs can suggest a creamier, more viscous spirit.

The Touch Ritual

In some Mexican traditions, you pour a few drops of mezcal into your palms and rub them together briskly. As the alcohol evaporates, you're left with the pure essence of the agave — no stickiness, no residue (since authentic mezcal has no additives).

03

The Taste — Besos, Mouthfeel & Flavor

Learning Objectives
  • Practice the "besos" (kisses) sipping technique
  • Identify front, middle, and finish flavors
  • Evaluate mouthfeel: creamy, oily, bright, crisp

Besos — The Art of Kissing Mezcal

Besos means "kisses" in Spanish. Sip the mezcal gently into the front of your mouth first, then "chew" it back over your tongue with control. This prevents the dreaded back-of-throat hit that can overwhelm even experienced drinkers.

Tasting the Journey: Front, Middle & Finish

Just like wine tasting, pay attention to how the flavor evolves. The front is your first impression, the middle develops as it spreads across your palate, and the finish is what lingers after you swallow. Great mezcal has a long, complex finish.

Mouthfeel

Is it creamy, lactic, oily, thick, thin, watery, bright, or crisp? Your brain cross-references the feel with the taste, so there's a lot going on. Take your time and let each sip tell its story.

Pro Tip

Your first sip awakens the palate. Subsequent sips often reveal new flavors. Don't judge a mezcal on the first taste alone.

04

The Practice — Tasting Notes & Building Your Palate

Learning Objectives
  • Develop your personal tasting vocabulary
  • Understand what makes mezcal flavors unique (terroir)
  • Know when to taste formally vs. simply enjoy

Finding Your Words

When tasting, blurt out the first words that come to mind — that's probably what you're tasting. "Cranberries," "wet earth," "eucalyptus," "smoke." There's no wrong answer. Over time, as you move through daily life, you'll notice aromas that remind you of mezcals you've tasted.

Purity & Terroir

Remember: mezcal is pure, with no additives. Everything you taste comes from the agave species, where it was grown, the local water source, wild yeast in the air, the fermentation vessel, and the firewood used for roasting and distillation. This is terroir in its most honest form.

Formal vs. Relaxed Tasting

Sometimes you want to analyze every note. Other times, you just want to follow the basic steps (pour, smell, besos) and kick back. You know you love a mezcal when you find yourself mindlessly reaching for that copita again and again. Both approaches are valid — match your style to the moment.

Pro Tip

Keep a tasting journal or use mezcalreviews.com to track your favorites. Your palate will evolve, and it's rewarding to look back on your journey.
